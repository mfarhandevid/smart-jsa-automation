Limitations

Overview



Smart JSA Automation is designed to support the JSA workflow through automation, AI-assisted analysis, structured data management, and digital communication.



However, the system has several limitations that should be considered when evaluating its capabilities and potential implementation.



Understanding these limitations is important to ensure that the system is used appropriately and that automation does not replace professional safety responsibilities.



AI Analysis Limitations



AI-assisted hazard analysis depends on the quality and completeness of the information provided to the system.



If the job information is incomplete, inaccurate, or unclear, the resulting analysis may also be incomplete or inaccurate.



The AI may also fail to identify:



* Site-specific hazards.
* Unusual or non-obvious hazards.
* Changing environmental conditions.
* Interactions between simultaneous activities.
* Equipment-specific conditions.
* Worker competency-related risks.



For this reason, AI-generated analysis should not be treated as a complete or final safety assessment.



Human Review Dependency



The system still depends on human review at critical stages.



The Safety Officer is required to review the AI-assisted analysis and JSA draft from a K3 perspective.



The HSSE Manager is responsible for the defined management-level review and approval process.



The automation does not independently determine whether a job is safe to perform.



Dependence on External Services



The workflow depends on external services and integrations.



Examples may include:



* WhatsApp communication services.
* n8n.
* Google Sheets.
* Google Docs.
* AI services.
* Other APIs or supporting integrations.



Changes to the availability, authentication requirements, API behavior, or pricing of these services may affect the workflow.



Data Quality Dependency



The reliability of the workflow depends on the quality of the data stored in the supporting data sources.



Incorrect or outdated information may affect:



* User validation.
* Role identification.
* Workflow routing.
* JSA registry records.
* Approval status.
* Document generation.



Data maintenance is therefore an important part of the system's operation.



Workflow Configuration Dependency



The workflow is designed around specific process rules, data structures, and approval logic.



Changes to the organization's:



* Roles.
* Approval hierarchy.
* JSA format.
* Data structure.
* Communication process.
* Safety procedures.



may require changes to the workflow configuration.



The system is therefore not necessarily a plug-and-play solution for every organization.



Operational Context Limitation



The system processes the information provided to it.



It does not automatically understand the complete real-world work environment unless relevant information is made available to the workflow.



Conditions at the worksite may change after the JSA has been prepared.



The actual work environment should therefore continue to be assessed according to the applicable organizational safety process.



Public Repository Limitations



The public repository does not contain all production-specific components of the original workflow.



Certain implementation details may be excluded to protect:



* Operational data.
* Personal information.
* Credentials.
* API keys.
* Private configuration.
* Organization-specific information.
* Sensitive safety documentation.



The public repository therefore demonstrates the system architecture, workflow design, and selected implementation concepts without exposing confidential operational information.



No Guarantee of Regulatory Compliance



The automation itself does not guarantee compliance with every applicable occupational safety requirement or regulation.



The system should be adapted to the organization's:



* Internal procedures.
* Applicable laws and regulations.
* Safety management system.
* Approval requirements.
* Operational conditions.



Professional review remains necessary to determine whether the resulting JSA meets the applicable requirements.



Summary



The Smart JSA Automation system is designed to support and streamline the JSA workflow.



Its effectiveness depends on:



* The quality of the input data.
* The reliability of external services.
* The accuracy of the workflow configuration.
* The quality of the AI-assisted analysis.
* The involvement of qualified human reviewers.
* The suitability of the system for the organization's operational context.



The system should therefore be viewed as a workflow automation and decision-support tool rather than a replacement for professional K3 judgment and organizational safety governance.
